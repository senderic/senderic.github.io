Recording & notes workflow — Google Recorder

Capture everything, transcribe on-device, summarize after. Full checklist lives in the trip goal’s recording-checklist.md; this is the short version from the plan.

  1. Set up before Monday (5 minutes)

    Google Recorder on the Pixel 9a: turn Speaker labels on (Pixel 6+, US English) and turn backup on so recordings also land at recorder.google.com. Free, no minute cap — unlike Otter free (300 min/month, 30 min per conversation).

  2. In the room

    Sit near the front, phone face-up on the table, hit record. Transcription is on-device, so it works in airplane mode and on dead conference Wi-Fi. Recordings can run up to 18 hours with the screen off. Recorder’s built-in AI summaries cover 5-minute to 1-hour recordings for a quick skim.

  3. Rename immediately

    Rename each recording with its session code (AIM407, COP401, Kathy dinner…) the moment it ends — future-you will not remember which “Recording 14” was which. Export via Share → Transcript (.txt) or straight to Google Docs.

  4. Summarize with Muse

    Send the audio or the .txt transcript to Muse for the real processing: clean summary, decisions, action items, and how it connects to your projects. If a transcript comes out garbled, send the raw audio instead.

  5. Etiquette & consent

    Nevada is one-party consent, so recording conversations you’re part of is legal. Breakouts are recorded by AWS anyway and personal audio notes are common — but skip anything marked confidential or under NDA, and for 1:1s a quick “mind if I record for notes?” costs nothing.

LiteLLM — there is no re:Invent session for it.

No session code exists for LiteLLM — don’t hunt the catalog for one. LiteLLM on Fargate + Bedrock Application Inference Profiles + Langfuse is an AWS-published architecture pattern (AWS ML Blog: “Building a self-service LLM gateway with Amazon Bedrock and LiteLLM”): Route 53 / CloudFront / ALB → LiteLLM on Fargate with a scoped IAM task role → Bedrock, per-user keys and cost controls, CloudWatch for RPM/TPM and quota alarms, and Langfuse for prompt-level telemetry. The booking playbook — act on the Nov 19 reality

The Storage guide’s arithmetic: the maximum possible 60-minute sessions across the whole week is 24 — with no travel, meals, or Expo. A sustainable plan is 3–4 sessions/day Mon–Thu (at most one 2-hour workshop), plus the Tue/Wed morning keynotes: roughly 14–17 live sessions for the week.

  1. Favorite everything on your target list — today

    Favorites drive change alerts and become your walk-up plan. Favorite freely, including overlapping sessions.

  2. Reserve any seat that’s actually open, in priority order

    One reservation per slot. Workshops and builders’ sessions first (smallest rooms), then chalk talks, then code talks.

  3. Hunt the repeats

    Look for -R / -R1 / -R2 sittings — many target sessions run twice (28 of 79 storage sessions repeat; AIM and IND397 repeat too). Verify each sitting separately: format, time, and venue can differ.

  4. Re-check daily — and after every keynote

    Cancellations reopen seats, and AWS adds new-launch sessions after each keynote. Search the catalog for “[NEW LAUNCH]” (e.g. DAT452/DAT453 slots at MGM Wed/Thu).

  5. Work the walk-up line like it’s the plan — because it is

    ~20% of capacity is never reserved, and every reserved no-show is released at T−10 minutes. For a must-see full chalk talk or workshop, queue 30–40 minutes early within the same venue cluster. For breakouts, just arrive early — they’re walk-up anyway.

  6. Protect every reservation you hold

    Arrive ≥10 minutes early or your seat is released and you go to the back of the walk-up line. And never hold a reservation you won’t use — release it in the app the moment a lane decision is made.

  7. Bring a personal laptop

    Workshops and many builders’ sessions require one, and corporate-device restrictions can interfere with hands-on labs.

Skip live — watch on demand

Partner Keynote · NET404 · CON326 · STG346 · DVT204 · CMP202 · any 200-level “what’s new” · introductory Bedrock, Lambda, ECS, or generic migration sessions. All breakouts are recorded.
